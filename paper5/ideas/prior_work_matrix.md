# Prior-Work Matrix for Low-Cost Post-Training LLM NAS

## Scope and audit rule

This matrix compares the 20-paper core library and seven narrowly targeted novelty checks. It deliberately ignores hardware-specific objectives except where a paper's software mechanism constrains novelty. “Calibration” means the data used to score or adapt a candidate, not downstream evaluation data. Search/training costs are qualitative unless the source reports a directly comparable quantity.

Targeted checks were added only because they could invalidate a candidate gap:

- [EvoPress](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/sieberling25a.html) (ICML 2025): tests whether global evolutionary compression already removes the independence/additivity gap.
- [AmoebaLLM](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/8f11e548311c7fd3f33596a4d1dd41f0-Abstract-Conference.html) (NeurIPS 2024): checks elastic arbitrary-depth/width subnets.
- [LPZero](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.502/) (Findings of EMNLP 2024): checks automated symbolic zero-cost proxies for language models.
- [DarwinLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07780) (COLM 2026): checks training-aware evolutionary pruning.
- [Building LLMs Like LEGO](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.2081/) (ACL 2026): checks block compatibility and lightweight interfaces for reassembly.
- [GPrune-LLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13418) (2026 **PREPRINT**): checks cross-distribution calibration bias.
- [Is C4 Dataset Optimal for Pruning?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07461) (2024 **PREPRINT**): checks whether calibration choice already has direct evidence.

No supplemental PDF was added to the core library in this turn; these primary pages are used only for novelty verification.

## Direct and highly related work

| Work | Problem and core assumption | Search space and unit | Search strategy and proxy | Calibration, training, recovery | Independence / interactions / bottleneck | Main contribution and failure modes | Saturated part and top-tier judgment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Search for Efficient Large Language Models** (NeurIPS 2024) | Extract a compact subnet from a pretrained LLM; assumes a small-sample language-model score plus inherited-weight repair is predictive. | Depth and attention/MLP row/column masks; layer/structured group. | Importance-initialized evolutionary search; candidate perplexity; ADMM weight reformation. | Few WikiText-2 samples; no candidate training during search; post-selection weight reformation. | Whole candidates are evaluated, but search has no explicit credit model for interactions or later recovery; repeated candidate inference is the bottleneck. | Establishes training-free pretrained-LLM NAS. Failure: calibration overfit and search expense. | “Training-free LLM subnet search” is saturated. A top-tier claim needs a new global estimator, decision process, or recovery objective—not another mask space. |
| **Puzzle** (ICML 2025) | Assemble an efficient child from a locally distilled block library; assumes replace-one-block scores add across the final model. | Per-position attention/FFN variants and skips; subblock/block. | Blockwise local distillation, one-time replace-one scoring, grouped-knapsack/MIP. | Large local distillation corpus; local scores; global KD after assembly. | Explicit local independence; child blocks see parent states, so assembled state drift and inter-block incompatibility are omitted. Cheap MIP is enabled by this assumption. | Scales decomposed post-training NAS to huge spaces. Failure: local rankings can change under child-generated inputs; global KD must repair composition. | Local library + additive assembly is saturated. Explicit composition error prediction remains open if it beats actual-candidate and global-proxy baselines. |
| **Jet-Nemotron** (NeurIPS 2025) | Convert a full-attention pretrained LLM to a heterogeneous efficient architecture; assumes a frozen/reused backbone and task-guided attention placement suffice. | Alternative attention paths and layer placement; attention layer/operator. | Once-for-all alternative paths plus task-dependent beam search. | Substantial distillation/continued training; recovery is integral and costly. | Placement is searched jointly enough to expose state dependence, but the recipe consumes tens to hundreds of billions of tokens. | Strong PostNAS precedent for operator placement. Failure: high recovery cost and unclear transfer of placement. | “Search where to retain attention” is saturated. A low-cost paper must change the scoring/recovery mechanism, not merely add operators. |
| **Týr-the-Pruner** (NeurIPS 2025) | Allocate non-uniform sparsity globally across heads and FFN channels; assumes expected error accumulation is a useful supernet proxy. | Head/FFN structured sparsity; groups and layerwise ratios. | Local Taylor/Hessian saliency, iterative supernet, coarse-to-fine evolution, global logits validation. | Calibration activations/gradients and about millions of tokens; no full candidate training in search. | Explicitly models inter-structure dependence through iterative construction/global validation, though it lacks an interpretable compositional error law. Search time remains high. | Strong global multi-axis allocation. Failure: expensive evolutionary evaluation and proxy/data dependence. | Non-uniform head/FFN allocation is saturated. Pairwise interactions alone are insufficient novelty. |
| **TraceNAS** (2026 **PREPRINT**) | Rank joint depth/width candidates without training; assumes gradient-trace correlation to the parent predicts adaptation/recovery. | Layer deletion plus attention/MLP width; whole architecture. | Evolutionary search with a whole-model, scale-invariant gradient-trace proxy. | 65,536-token calibration reported; zero-shot search, then large continued pretraining in main results. | Chain-rule gradient captures global dependence, but the score is immediate/first-order and does not model finite-horizon recovery or curvature. | Direct global zero-shot and recovery-alignment threat. Failure: fixed-budget search and expensive gradient scoring. | “Global rather than local zero-cost LLM proxy” is saturated. New work must identify a missing quantity such as terminal recovery dynamics. |
| **Distill-then-Replace** (2026 **PREPRINT**) | Build a task-specific attention hybrid from a pretrained model; assumes locally distilled replacements can be inserted greedily if validation remains acceptable. | Full-to-linear attention replacements; attention block. | Local distillation then greedy replacement with whole-model task validation after each acceptance. | Task data; about 100M-token local distillation scale; no final large search training. | Re-evaluation captures current-prefix effects but remains myopic and path-dependent. | Demonstrates single-A800 local replacement. Failure: task specificity and greedy local optimum. | Local distill-and-greedily-replace is saturated; a new method needs non-myopic/global compositional reasoning. |
| **BlockPruner** (Findings of ACL 2025) | Remove fine-grained residual attention/MLP blocks with no training; assumes iterative perplexity identifies the next safe deletion. | MHA/MLP residual blocks; sublayer. | Greedy iterative deletion and perplexity recomputation. | Small calibration set; no training or recovery. | Captures changing marginal importance by recomputation, but only along one irreversible greedy path. Cost grows with candidates × steps. | Strong simple interaction-aware baseline. Failure: local minima and calibration sensitivity. | Iterative local score recomputation is saturated; any sequential method must beat greedy with equal evaluations. |
| **SLEB** (ICML 2024) | Remove redundant Transformer blocks; assumes whole-model perplexity after each tentative removal is the right marginal score. | Full Transformer blocks; layer. | Iterative greedy verification/elimination. | 128 calibration samples reported; no recovery. | Directly proves importance changes after previous deletions; handles context dependence but no lookahead. | Canonical dynamic layer-pruning baseline. Failure: destructive path dependence. | “Importance is context dependent” is known. Novelty requires a non-myopic solution, not rediscovery. |
| **LLM-Pruner** (NeurIPS 2023) | Prune coupled structures while preserving dependencies; assumes first-order/Taylor group saliency predicts damage. | Heads, channels and coupled groups; dependency graph groups. | Dependency discovery and gradient/Taylor importance. | Small calibration set; LoRA recovery. | Structural coupling is represented, but functional cross-group interactions and recovery outcomes are not. | Establishes dependency-aware LLM structured pruning. Failure: static saliency can misrank aggressive compositions. | Structural dependency graphs and LoRA recovery are saturated as components. |
| **Minitron** (NeurIPS 2024) | Derive compact models across several axes; assumes lightweight retraining can reveal a good pruned architecture. | Depth, heads, MLP width, embedding width; architecture configurations. | Activation importance plus small feasible-set enumeration. | Each candidate receives substantial lightweight retraining (about 1.8B tokens in the described selection study); KD recovery. | Empirically shows rankings can change after recovery; exhaustive candidate recovery is the bottleneck. | Strong evidence that pre- and post-recovery quality differ. Failure: too costly as a general search evaluator. | Multi-axis pruning and retrain-to-rank are saturated. Cheap prediction of terminal quality remains open. |
| **SliceGPT** (ICLR 2024) | Compress dense dimensions without irregular sparsity; exploits computational invariance and PCA structure. | Hidden dimension/rows/columns; global width slices. | Closed-form/activation-derived rotations and slicing, not combinatorial NAS. | Calibration activations; optional recovery depending setup. | Avoids combinatorial interactions by a mathematically constrained transformation. | Strong algebraic baseline with standard dense execution. Failure: restricted family of width reductions. | Any width-search paper must beat this simple, principled baseline. |
| **Sheared LLaMA** (ICLR 2024) | Produce a target architecture from a larger pretrained LLM; assumes learned masks plus continued pretraining recover a fixed target shape. | Depth, heads, hidden and FFN dimensions; global target architecture. | Targeted structured pruning and dynamic-batch-loading continued pretraining. | Heavy pruning stage and ~50B-token continued pretraining. | Joint mask learning captures some coupling, but recovery dominates cost. | Strong target-shape and data-mixture baseline. Failure: incompatible with a genuinely low-cost search budget. | Fixed target architecture shearing is saturated; low-cost work must not hide large recovery. |
| **MultiPruner** (2025 **PREPRINT**) | Balance removal across blocks, heads and channels; assumes a staged dimension order plus local scores approximates joint allocation. | Residual blocks, MLP channels, heads; multi-axis groups. | Iterative staged pruning. | Calibration inference; no large search training. | Cross-axis competition is handled heuristically/sequentially, not as one causal decision model. | Practical low-cost multi-axis baseline. Failure: dimension-order dependence. | Merely unifying axes is saturated. |
| **NAS-BERT** (KDD 2021) | Obtain task-agnostic adaptive-size BERT students; assumes total student quality can be approximated by blockwise losses. | Block/operator/depth/width choices; Transformer blocks. | Blockwise distillation, search-space pruning, additive performance approximation. | Teacher data and supernet/block training; downstream adaptation later. | Strong local independence/additivity assumption. | Early blockwise Transformer NAS precedent. Failure: encoder scale and block-score mismatch. | Blockwise additive estimation and multi-size students are old. |
| **AutoDistil** (NeurIPS 2022) | Search task-agnostic distilled Transformer students cheaply; assumes subspace-specific supernets and attention loss rank inherited subnets. | Depth/width/head configurations; subnet. | Few-shot SuperLMs, in-place attention distillation, held-out self-attention ranking. | Supernet training is required; no per-candidate full training. | Weight-sharing interference is reduced by partitioning but not removed. | Strong distillation-aware supernet baseline. Failure: proxy-to-task ranking and encoder-scale transfer. | A new surrogate must address pretrained decoder surgery rather than replay supernet NAS. |
| **LANA** (ECCV 2022) | Transform a teacher by locally training alternative operations and globally selecting them; assumes layerwise accuracy changes add. | Per-layer operator library; layer replacement. | Teacher-guided local replacement plus integer programming. | Local teacher-feature distillation; selected model fine-tuning. | Ignores cross-layer compatibility at search time. | Foundational local-distill/global-assemble method. Failure: local score non-compositionality. | The general block-library recipe is saturated and is adjacent only; hardware scope is excluded here. |
| **Once-for-All** (ICLR 2020) | Amortize many deployment architectures in one network; assumes progressive shrinking yields rank-faithful inherited subnets. | Depth, width, kernel/resolution; nested subnet. | Elastic supernet, progressive shrinking, learned accuracy predictor. | Expensive up-front training; 16K-subnet predictor data in the original setting. | Weight-sharing bias and interference; cheap later queries. | Canonical nested multi-budget baseline. Failure: requires purpose-built supernet. | “One model for many budgets” is saturated broadly; post-training coherent paths need a stronger distinction. |
| **NASWOT** (ICML 2021) | Rank untrained networks without training; assumes activation-pattern diversity at initialization tracks trainability. | Generic cell networks; whole architecture. | Activation binary-code kernel log-determinant. | One/few minibatches; no training. | No pretrained inheritance or recovery model. | Canonical zero-cost proxy. Failure: domain/scale transfer. | Applying NASWOT unchanged to LLMs is not novelty. |
| **TE-NAS** (ICLR 2021) | Cheaply rank untrained architectures through trainability and expressivity; assumes NTK condition and linear regions capture quality. | Generic NAS spaces; architecture. | Pruning-by-importance using NTK condition number and region count. | Few batches; no training. | No pretrained state, structured surgery, or terminal recovery. | Theoretically motivated zero-cost baseline. | A pretrained-LLM proxy needs a distinct derivation and strong transfer evidence. |
| **ZiCo** (ICLR 2023) | Rank architectures by gradient stability; assumes inverse gradient coefficient of variation predicts convergence/generalization. | Generic networks; architecture. | Gradient-statistics zero-shot score. | Small batches; no training. | Aggregate score can hide module coupling; not recovery-aware. | Strong gradient proxy baseline. | Merely replacing perplexity with gradient CV is weak novelty. |

## Supplemental novelty threats

| Work | Why it was needed | What it covers | Remaining boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EvoPress** (ICML 2025) | A generic “interactions invalidate additive pruning” claim looked too easy. | Explicitly rejects independent/additive layer errors and uses global evolutionary compression with low evaluation complexity across structural pruning, sparsity, and quantization. | It does not provide a compositional functional-error estimator or recovery dynamics, but it is a killer baseline for global search. |
| **AmoebaLLM** (NeurIPS 2024) | A cross-budget/nested idea risked duplicating elastic LLM search. | Arbitrary depth/width subnets from one fine-tuned model, with depth shrinkage, width importance, shape-aware LoRA, and in-place distillation. | It trains an elastic family; it does not optimize a post-training path with integrated cross-budget regret under no supernet training. |
| **LPZero** (EMNLP 2024) | “Discover a better LLM zero-cost formula” risked being already automated. | Genetic programming discovers symbolic zero-cost proxies and evaluates language-model search spaces. | It does not model terminal recovery or architecture-specific calibration uncertainty. |
| **DarwinLM** (COLM 2026) | Recovery-aware NAS may already exist. | Evolves structured-pruning candidates and performs lightweight multistep training inside progressive offspring selection. | It pays candidate-training cost rather than predicting finite-horizon dynamics analytically; it is nevertheless the killer baseline. |
| **Building LLMs Like LEGO** (ACL 2026) | A compatibility-adapter fallback risked direct duplication. | Two-dimensional block reassembly and lightweight glue layers trained by data-free KD to handle representation incompatibility. | Glue layers are covered; predicting unadapted composition damage from transported residuals remains distinct. |
| **GPrune-LLM** (2026 **PREPRINT**) | Multi-domain robust pruning appeared open. | Measures cross-distribution neuron behavior, reorganizes ranking spaces, adapts scores, and learns module sparsity under a global budget. | Generic “use multiple calibration domains” is covered. Architecture-level selection uncertainty and paired robust ranking are narrower remaining gaps. |
| **Is C4 Dataset Optimal for Pruning?** (2024 **PREPRINT**) | Calibration sensitivity needed direct evidence. | Demonstrates large and unintuitive effects of calibration-data choice across pruning methods/tasks. | It diagnoses/selects data rather than solving robust architecture ranking under limited calibration. |

## Cross-paper conclusions

1. **Local-to-global mismatch is real but crowded.** Puzzle, LANA, and NAS-BERT expose additive assumptions; SLEB and BlockPruner adaptively recompute; Týr, TraceNAS, and EvoPress already claim global dependence. A viable paper needs a new estimator with measurable rank/cost benefit, not the observation alone.
2. **Recovery awareness is empirically important and no longer untouched.** Minitron exhibits post-recovery rank changes, TraceNAS predicts recovery alignment, and DarwinLM trains candidates during selection. The remaining defensible space is terminal finite-horizon prediction at far lower cost.
3. **Multi-axis and non-uniform search are saturated as high-level claims.** LLM-Pruner, Sheared LLaMA, Minitron, MultiPruner, Týr, and TraceNAS all cover them.
4. **Calibration robustness is now a narrow opportunity.** Calibration sensitivity is established, and GPrune-LLM covers cross-distribution neuron scoring. Only architecture-level, selection-aware robust ranking remains plausibly distinct.
5. **Block compatibility adapters are covered.** Puzzle repairs globally and Building LLMs Like LEGO trains glue layers. A novel block-composition direction must predict or optimize propagated errors before expensive repair.
